2. What we process

Account

Signing up requires an email address and a password. The password is never stored in plain text, only as a cryptographic hash — we cannot read it either. In addition: a display name (pre-filled from the part before the @ when you register, changeable at any time), optionally a profile picture, and the time of registration.

The email address must be confirmed before the account can be used. We send a single message containing an activation link for that.

Recipes and everything attached to them

For every recipe we store the title, ingredients, preparation steps, the chosen category, an image and — for imported recipes — the source URL and the author details published by the network in question (username, display name, profile link). Those details stay with the recipe so its origin remains visible.

We also temporarily store the original text of the post and, where produced, a transcript of the audio. They exist solely so a failed recipe can be re-analysed without fetching the post again, and are deleted automatically after 90 days (see Retention).

Beyond that: your weekly plan, your favourites, your categories, and any photos you take or pick to illustrate a recipe.

Links between cookbooks

If you share your cookbook with someone, we store the link between the two accounts as well as the invitation code created for it, its expiry time and who redeemed it.

Imports and subscription

We count how many imports your account has used and store whether a subscription exists and until when. We do not process payment data — the purchase runs entirely through Google Play or the App Store (see Recipients).

Technical data

Accessing our servers produces the usual connection data (IP address, timestamp, endpoint called, status code). It serves operation and troubleshooting. We use no analytics or tracking tools, there is no advertising, and we do not build usage profiles.

5. Transfers outside the EU

The database and images are held in the EU (Supabase, Ireland) — no transfer to a third country takes place there.

Google and RevenueCat, by contrast, are based or host servers in the United States. Transfers rely on the EU-US Data Privacy Framework where the provider is certified under it, and otherwise on the European Commission's Standard Contractual Clauses under Art. 46(2)(c) GDPR. Despite those safeguards, access by US authorities cannot be ruled out.

7. Shared cookbooks

When someone redeems your invitation code, both sides can see each other's recipes and categories as well as the other's display name and profile picture. The weekly plan is kept jointly. Favourites stay private and are never shared.

Other people's recipes can be viewed and cooked, but not edited or deleted. You can undo the link at any time in the settings; the other side's recipes then disappear from your view.

8. Third-party content from TikTok and Instagram

When you import a link, we fetch the publicly available post and store recipe details, a preview image and the post's author information from it. This may involve personal data of third parties — for example the name of the person who published the post. The legal basis is Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR; our legitimate interest is keeping a recipe traceable to its source.

Preview images are not publicly accessible. They are held in closed storage and served only to signed-in accounts allowed to see the recipe in question.

If you are the author of a post and do not want its content processed in Kochbuch, write to info@juicyslaedle.de — we will remove it and block the source from future imports.

9. What stays on your device

Two things you would normally expect in the cloud run locally here:

  • Preparation by the language model, where your device provides one (Gemini Nano on newer Android devices, Apple Intelligence on iPhones). Google is asked only when that is missing.
  • Translation of foreign-language recipes into your device language.

Your device also holds a copy of your recipes so the app starts without waiting. It is erased when you sign out.

10. Your rights

You have the right to:

  • rectification of inaccurate data (Art. 16 GDPR)
  • erasure (Art. 17 GDPR) — in the app under Settings → Profile → Delete account, or here without the app
  • restriction of processing (Art. 18 GDPR)
  • data portability (Art. 20 GDPR)
  • object to processing based on Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR (Art. 21 GDPR)

An informal message to info@juicyslaedle.de is enough.

11. This website

These pages are static and served through GitHub Pages (GitHub Inc., USA). On access, GitHub processes the usual connection data including the IP address; we have no influence over that.

We set no cookies, embed no third-party fonts, maps or analytics, and measure nothing. The typeface is served from our own site so that merely opening a page sends no data to third parties.
